Overview

My research connects probabilistic theory, statistical inference, and computation for Hawkes processes. These models describe event sequences in which past events influence the rate of future activity.

My current work includes likelihood-based inference, oscillatory excitation kernels, analytic moment formulas, and computational tools for simulation and estimation. The manuscripts below are in preparation or ready for submission; they are not listed as accepted or published articles.

  • Non-monotonic Hawkes Processes: Theory, Inference, and Applications
  • Analytic Moment Formulas for Hawkes Processes via Differential Operators
  • Utilizing the Hawkes Process to Apply Orthogonality in Normed Linear Space
  • R Software for Hawkes Process Simulation and Inference

Non-monotonic Hawkes Processes: Theory, Inference, and Applications

Status: Manuscript in preparation.

This project develops likelihood-based inference for parametric Hawkes processes, with particular attention to sinusoidally modulated, exponentially decaying excitation kernels. These kernels allow the influence of an event to exhibit recurring peaks while decaying over time.

Likelihood theory

I study the relationship between stationary Hawkes processes and processes observed over a finite time interval. Under appropriate regularity, identifiability, and integrability conditions, the analysis establishes consistency and asymptotic normality of maximum likelihood estimators. The framework states assumptions in terms of the excitation kernel, allowing applications beyond the particular oscillatory model.

Estimation and Fisher information

For the oscillatory model, I investigate Fisher information and develop analytic expressions and approximations to support uncertainty quantification. A multistage estimation procedure combines frequency exploration with local likelihood optimization. Simulation studies examine parameter recovery and the accuracy of estimated uncertainty.

Testing for oscillatory structure

Another component considers testing for sinusoidal modulation. The frequency parameter is not identified under the null hypothesis of no modulation, requiring an approach that accounts for this feature. I investigate a global score-based test across candidate frequencies, using a Davies-type approach to approximate significance.

Applications to financial event data explore when oscillatory excitation provides useful information beyond models with monotonically decaying kernels.

Analytic Moment Formulas for Hawkes Processes via Differential Operators

Status: Manuscript in preparation.

This project develops a differential operator approach to moment calculations for linear Hawkes processes. The central idea is to express the effect of the excitation kernel through a linear differential operator and reduce the associated moment equations to ordinary differential equations.

A common framework for several kernel families

The approach applies to kernels that admit a suitable finite-order linear differential representation. I investigate exponential, gamma-type, and oscillatory kernels within this framework, using cluster factorial moments to organize the calculations.

For the exponential kernel, the method recovers known moment formulas. Extensions to gamma-type and oscillatory kernels provide a systematic route to analytic or semi-analytic expressions for event-count moments.

Purpose and further directions

The goal is to make moment calculations more systematic across kernel families and clarify how kernel parameters influence event-count variability. Further work considers higher-order moments and their use in model comparison and diagnostic methods.

R Software for Hawkes Process Simulation and Inference

Status: In development; documentation in progress.

I am developing R tools for simulating Hawkes processes, estimating model parameters, and examining model fit. This work supports reproducible numerical experiments and comparisons between excitation kernels.

  • Simulation of event sequences.
  • Likelihood-based parameter estimation.
  • Diagnostic and model comparison tools.
  • Examples and documentation for reproducible analysis.

A repository link will be added when the software is ready for public release.

Connections to Student Projects

These research directions offer opportunities for students to explore stochastic models through simulation and data analysis. Accessible starting points include comparing Poisson and Hawkes processes, investigating event clustering, and studying estimation accuracy using simulated data.

At Augusta University, I have presented a proposed master's capstone project on Hawkes process forecasting, with potential applications to neural spike trains and cybersecurity event logs.
