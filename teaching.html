Teaching Philosophy

My goal is to help students understand mathematical ideas and become more independent problem solvers. Four principles guide how I explain concepts, design activities, and respond to students.

  • Clarity. I introduce ideas through approachable examples, connect them to prior knowledge, and explain the reasoning behind each step. Clear expectations and organized materials help students focus on learning.
  • Engagement. Students need opportunities to work through problems, explain their thinking, and compare approaches. Guided practice, short questions, and group activities make their understanding visible.
  • Inclusion. I aim to create a classroom where students can ask questions without embarrassment. I provide different ways to participate and make time for individual conversations.
  • Growth. Frequent feedback helps students recognize what they understand and what needs more practice. I encourage them to examine mistakes, adjust their study habits, and build understanding over time.

These principles guide my work in both classroom and online settings. The tools may differ, but students still need clear explanations, meaningful practice, timely feedback, and access to support.

Classroom Practices and Student Support

  • Organized course materials. I provide clearly arranged notes, assignments, practice materials, and deadlines so students can plan their work.
  • Short checks for understanding. Concept questions and quizzes help identify gaps in prerequisite knowledge and topics that need additional explanation.
  • Guided practice and review. Worked examples, practice tests, and review activities help students connect ideas and prepare for independent problem solving.
  • Collaborative projects. Group projects encourage students to apply course concepts and explain their reasoning to others.
  • Early outreach. I monitor participation, assignments, and assessment results and contact students who may need support.
  • Individual conversations. One-to-one meetings provide space to address questions, discuss study strategies, and encourage students to persist.
  • Purposeful technology. I use D2L Brightspace, Pearson MyLab Math, Microsoft Forms, and other teaching tools to organize learning, gather feedback, and support practice.

Proposed Master's Capstone

I presented When Events Breed Events: A Capstone Project Pitch to master's students at Augusta University. The proposed project explores Hawkes process modeling and probabilistic forecasting, with potential applications to neural spike trains and cybersecurity event logs.

The proposed plan covers simulation, data preparation, parameter estimation, comparison with a Poisson baseline, forecast evaluation, and a final report and presentation. This is a proposed mentoring opportunity rather than a completed supervised capstone.

Potential Undergraduate Research Projects
